How Business Value Is Determined in Montana

Business valuation is both art and science. Unlike commercial real estate where cap rates provide a clear framework, business value involves multiple methods — and the right one depends on your industry, profitability, growth trajectory, and what buyers in your category are paying.

Most Common Method

Seller's Discretionary Earnings (SDE)

Used for owner-operated businesses. Takes net profit and adds back owner salary, depreciation, and one-time expenses to show true cash flow. Buyers pay a multiple of SDE — typically 2x–4x depending on industry and risk.

Larger Businesses

EBITDA Multiple

Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization. Used for businesses with $1M+ in revenue and management teams that don't depend solely on the owner. Multiples vary by industry and growth.

Asset-Heavy Businesses

Asset Valuation

When the business's primary value is in its physical assets — equipment, inventory, real property — rather than its earnings. Common in manufacturing, construction, and asset-intensive service businesses.

Market Approach

Comparable Sales

What similar businesses have actually sold for in Montana and comparable markets. This market data is essential context for any valuation method and helps set realistic expectations for both buyers and sellers.

What buyers are actually paying in Montana right now:

Main Street businesses (restaurants, retail, service) typically trade at 2x–3x SDE. Businesses with strong recurring revenue, transferable systems, and reduced owner dependency command higher multiples. Businesses that are heavily owner-dependent — where the owner IS the business — trade at lower multiples or struggle to sell at all. This is one of the most important things to understand before deciding when to sell.

The Business Sale Process

1

Confidential Consultation

We start privately — no public exposure, no employees finding out. We discuss your business, your goals, your timeline, and what a successful outcome looks like for you.

2

Business Valuation & Financial Normalization

We work through your financials to establish a defensible asking price. This includes normalizing owner compensation, identifying add-backs, and presenting the business's true earnings picture to buyers.

3

Confidential Marketing Package

We prepare a professional Confidential Business Review (CBR) — a document that gives qualified buyers everything they need to evaluate the opportunity without revealing the business's identity upfront.

4

Qualified Buyer Outreach

We reach the right buyers — local entrepreneurs, out-of-state investors, strategic acquirers, and SBA-qualified buyers — under NDA before any identifying information is shared.

5

Offer Negotiation & Due Diligence

We negotiate terms, manage the due diligence process, and keep the transaction moving. Most deals that fall apart do so in due diligence — experienced representation here is critical.

6

Closing & Transition

A successful business sale closes with a well-structured purchase agreement, a clear transition plan, and both parties protected. We coordinate with attorneys, accountants, and lenders to get you to the finish line.
