What Is a Commercial Real Estate Broker — and How Are They Different from a Residential Agent?

A commercial real estate broker is a licensed real estate professional who specializes in income-producing properties, investment real estate, business properties, and commercial leasing — as opposed to residential agents who primarily handle homes and condominiums.

The distinction matters because commercial transactions require a fundamentally different skill set. Commercial brokers must understand income-based valuation, cap rate analysis, lease structures, tenant credit evaluation, 1031 exchange mechanics, and the ability to navigate complex multi-party transactions that can take 6 to 12 months to close. Using a residential agent for a commercial transaction — even a well-intentioned and capable one — is one of the most expensive mistakes a property owner or investor can make.

In Missoula, this distinction is especially important. There are relatively few brokers with deep commercial experience across all property types. Identifying the right one — and understanding what to look for — is the starting point for any successful commercial transaction.

The bottom line: Commercial real estate is not a specialty within residential real estate. It is a separate discipline. Verify that any broker you consider has a track record of closed commercial transactions — not just a commercial license endorsement on a primarily residential practice.

What Questions Should You Ask a Commercial Real Estate Broker Before Hiring?

Before you sign a listing agreement or buyer representation agreement with any commercial broker, ask these questions:

  • How many commercial transactions have you closed in Missoula in the past 3 years — and in what property categories? A broker who has closed 5 industrial deals and 2 multifamily sales is not the same as one who has handled 50 transactions across every asset class.
  • Do you represent buyers, sellers, or both in the same transaction? Dual agency — representing both sides of a deal — creates a fundamental conflict of interest. Understand whose interests your broker is actually representing.
  • What is your access to the active buyer pool in Missoula? The best commercial deals are not won on marketing brochures — they're won on direct relationships with qualified buyers. Ask who specifically the broker knows who might be interested in your property.
  • What is your experience with properties like mine specifically? A broker with deep multifamily experience may not be the right fit for a net lease retail property or a Montana liquor license transaction.
  • How do you determine pricing? Be wary of any broker who leads with an aggressive number to win the listing. Ask to see the comparable transaction data that supports their valuation.

What Does a Commercial Real Estate Broker Actually Do?

A full-service commercial broker provides significantly more than a listing and a lockbox. In a properly handled commercial transaction, your broker is responsible for:

  • Market analysis and pricing strategy based on actual comparable transactions
  • Property marketing — financial summary, offering memorandum, targeted buyer outreach
  • Qualifying interested buyers before exposing confidential financial information
  • Offer evaluation — analyzing price, terms, contingencies, and closing timeline together, not just the headline number
  • Due diligence management — coordinating the buyer's inspection, financial review, and legal review to keep the transaction moving
  • Lender and attorney coordination through closing
  • Problem-solving when issues surface — and they always do

The difference between an experienced commercial broker and an inexperienced one shows most clearly in that last point. Commercial transactions rarely close without complications. Lease assignment issues, title complications, environmental concerns, appraisal gaps, lender conditions, and due diligence surprises are routine. A broker who has navigated hundreds of these situations knows how to keep a deal alive when a less experienced broker might watch it fall apart.

Why Local Market Knowledge Matters More in Missoula Than in Larger Markets

In a major market — Denver, Seattle, Phoenix — there are hundreds of experienced commercial brokers, deep transaction databases, and robust public data on comparable sales. A capable out-of-market broker can get up to speed quickly.

Missoula does not work that way. The commercial market is small enough that personal relationships are the primary driver of outcomes. Off-market transactions — deals done before a property ever appears on a public listing platform — are common. The buyer for a specific property may be an investor who has been working with the same local broker for 15 years and who mentioned what they were looking for in a conversation last month.

That kind of market intelligence is not available in a database. It is built through years of being present in the market, doing deals, and maintaining relationships with the active buyer and tenant pool. It is why a broker with 34 years of Missoula commercial real estate experience has a fundamentally different access and capability set than one who has been in the market for 3 years — regardless of how capable they are in other respects.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a commercial real estate broker and a residential agent?

Commercial brokers specialize in income-producing and investment properties, business real estate, and commercial leasing. The skill set — income-based valuation, cap rate analysis, complex lease structures, multi-party transactions — is fundamentally different from residential sales. Using a residential agent for a commercial transaction typically results in significant value left on the table.

How do I find a good commercial real estate broker in Missoula?

Ask for a verified list of closed commercial transactions in Missoula — not just listings or active inventory. A broker's track record of actually closing deals, across the specific property types you're interested in, is the most reliable indicator of capability. Personal referrals from other commercial property owners in the market are also valuable.

How much does a commercial real estate broker charge in Missoula?

Commercial brokerage commissions in Montana are negotiable and vary by transaction type, size, and complexity. For property sales, commissions are typically paid by the seller and range from 4 to 6 percent of the sale price. For leasing, commissions are typically paid by the landlord and calculated on total lease value. Buyer and tenant representation is often provided at no cost to the buyer or tenant.

Do I need a separate broker for business sales and commercial real estate?

Not necessarily. Many commercial transactions in Missoula involve both real property and a business — a restaurant with a liquor license, a retail operation with its own building, or a medical practice with real estate. A broker experienced in both commercial real estate and business brokerage can handle these transactions as a single integrated process, which is typically faster and produces better outcomes than coordinating two separate brokers.
