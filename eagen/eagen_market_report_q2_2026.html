Q2 2026 Market Snapshot

The Missoula commercial real estate market continued its measured pace through Q2 2026. Transaction volume remained steady, with investment-grade properties attracting strong interest from both regional and out-of-state 1031 exchange buyers. Sellers in well-positioned asset classes — particularly multifamily and net lease retail — continued to see competitive offers.

Office and older retail saw continued pricing pressure as tenants prioritize modern, flexible space. Industrial and land held firm on the strength of limited supply and continued development interest in the Rattlesnake, Reserve Street, and Brooks Street corridors.

Multifamily — Apartment Market

Missoula's apartment market remained one of the most watched asset classes in Q2. Rent growth has moderated from the aggressive pace of 2021–2023, but occupancy in established neighborhoods (University District, Rattlesnake, Central Missoula) held above 95%.

New construction deliveries have added supply pressure in some submarkets, but Class B and C properties — particularly those with renovation upside — attracted significant investor attention from buyers unable to compete for Class A assets in larger markets.

Retail & Net Lease

Retail performance in Missoula was bifurcated in Q2. National-credit, net lease properties — particularly those anchored by essential service tenants (medical, grocery, quick service restaurant) — continued to trade at aggressive cap rates as investors sought predictable, passive income streams.

Older strip retail and non-credit single-tenant properties faced more scrutiny, with buyers requiring higher yield thresholds or renovation concessions to account for lease rollover risk.

Office Market

Missoula's office market continued to navigate the post-pandemic recalibration that has defined the sector nationally. Vacancy climbed to approximately 12% — up from 9% in early 2024 — as several tenants downsized or shifted to hybrid arrangements requiring less dedicated space.

Medical office remained the clear bright spot. Healthcare-adjacent tenants continued to sign longer leases and showed minimal appetite to reduce footprint. Downtown Class A office with modern amenities and parking maintained stronger occupancy than suburban Class B product.

For office property owners: If you're holding older Class B office and considering your options, the current window — before further vacancy pressure — may represent an optimal time to evaluate a sale strategy. Buyers willing to take on lease-up risk exist, but they require yield compensation. A confidential valuation conversation costs nothing.

Industrial & Flex Space

Industrial remained the tightest segment of the Missoula commercial market in Q2 2026. Vacancy held below 4% — effectively full — and asking rents for available space continued to rise as tenants competed for limited options.

New industrial development has been constrained by land costs, construction pricing, and limited sites with appropriate zoning and access. This supply constraint has been a consistent support for industrial values and is expected to persist through 2026 and into 2027.

1031 Exchange Buyers — What This Means for Missoula Sellers

One of the most significant demand drivers in the Missoula market right now is the continued flow of 1031 exchange capital from larger, more expensive markets — primarily California, Washington, and Oregon.

These buyers have typically sold a property in a high-cost market and are reinvesting proceeds to defer capital gains taxes. They need to identify a replacement property within 45 days and close within 180 days. This time pressure makes them motivated, pre-qualified, and willing to pay market or above for well-positioned assets.

Why this matters if you're thinking about selling: 1031 buyers are not looking for a deal — they're looking for a clean, well-documented asset they can close quickly. If your property is stabilized, leased, and in a desirable submarket, you may be able to command a premium that wouldn't exist with a local buyer pool. This is the market window Missoula sellers should be paying attention to.

Q3 2026 Outlook

What to Watch Through the Rest of 2026

Interest rates remain the largest variable in the Missoula CRE equation. The Federal Reserve's posture through H2 2026 will directly influence cap rate compression or expansion across all asset classes. Sellers who move in Q3 — before potential rate uncertainty increases buyer caution — may be in an advantageous position.

Multifamily: Expect continued strong demand. New supply coming online may moderate rent growth, but investment demand remains robust.

Retail: National-credit NNN will hold firm. Multi-tenant retail with vacancy faces continued headwinds.

Office: Continued softening likely. Medical office the exception. Sellers with older Class B product should evaluate options now.

Industrial: Vacancy expected to stay tight through year-end. Rent growth likely to continue.

Land: Development interest remains high, particularly along South Missoula and infill sites near downtown. Limited inventory supporting values.
