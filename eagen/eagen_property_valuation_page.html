How Commercial Property Is Valued in Missoula

Commercial property valuation is fundamentally different from residential. Your neighbor's house sale has almost no bearing on what your commercial building is worth. Commercial values are driven by income, market cap rates, replacement cost, and comparable sales — and the weighting of each depends on the property type.

Here's how the three primary valuation approaches work:

01
Income Approach

The most common method for income-producing properties. Value is derived by dividing the property's Net Operating Income (NOI) by the prevailing market cap rate. A property generating $60,000 NOI at a 6% cap rate is worth approximately $1,000,000.

02
Sales Comparison

Value is benchmarked against recent sales of comparable properties in the same market. This approach works best when there are sufficient comparable transactions — which requires genuine market knowledge, not just MLS access.

03
Cost Approach

Estimates what it would cost to replace the property at today's construction costs, minus depreciation, plus land value. Most applicable for special-use properties or new construction where income data is limited.

What online estimates get wrong: Automated valuation tools (Zillow, LoopNet estimates, AI tools) apply broad algorithms that don't account for Missoula's specific submarket dynamics, lease terms, tenant quality, local cap rate compression, or the 1031 buyer demand currently active in this market. A number from an algorithm is not a valuation — it's a starting point for a conversation.

Factors That Affect Your Property's Value

No two commercial properties are identical. Here are the variables that most significantly influence what a buyer will pay for your asset in the current Missoula market:

Lease Terms & Tenant Quality

Long-term leases with creditworthy tenants command premium pricing. Month-to-month or near-expiry leases introduce risk that buyers price in.

Current Occupancy

Stabilized, fully-leased properties trade at tighter cap rates than vacant or partially-occupied buildings. Vacancy is the single biggest value detractor.

Location & Submarket

Reserve Street, University District, and downtown Missoula command different pricing than rural or secondary locations. Submarket matters enormously.

Property Condition

Deferred maintenance, aging mechanical systems, and roof condition all reduce buyer appetite or require price concessions to compensate.

Market Cap Rates

Cap rates in Missoula shift with interest rates and investor demand. In Q2 2026, cap rate compression from 1031 buyer activity is supporting values.

Recent Comparable Sales

What similar properties have sold for in the past 6–12 months in Missoula is the most direct market signal. This data requires access, not a Google search.

National credit tenants trading at lower cap rates. Local / regional tenants at higher yields reflecting perceived risk.

Wider range reflecting uncertainty. Medical office tighter. Older Class B office seeing upward cap rate pressure.

Industrial / Flex

Tight vacancy supporting values. Single-tenant net lease industrial at lower end of range.

Mixed Use

6.5% – 8.0%

Depends heavily on income mix, occupancy, and location. Blended rate between retail and residential components.

Development Land

Priced per SF / Acre

Land value driven by entitlements, zoning, location, and development potential rather than income capitalization.

What this means in dollars: If you own a stabilized apartment building generating $80,000 in annual NOI, at a 6% cap rate that property is worth approximately $1,333,000. At a 5.5% cap rate — which active 1031 buyer demand has supported in Q2 — that same income stream is worth $1,454,000. A half-point in cap rate on that asset is a $121,000 difference in value.
