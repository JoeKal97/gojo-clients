Montana Liquor License Sales & Transfers

Montana issues a limited number of liquor licenses, which means existing licenses carry significant market value. Unlike most states, Montana restricts new license issuance — so buyers who want to operate a licensed establishment must acquire an existing license through a transfer process regulated by the Montana Department of Revenue.

All-Beverages License

Full Liquor License

The most valuable and sought-after license type in Montana. Allows sale of beer, wine, and spirits. Quota-controlled by county population, creating significant scarcity value in growing markets like Missoula.

Beer & Wine License

Beer & Wine Only

Less restrictive quota than all-beverages licenses but still subject to transfer regulations. Common in restaurants, taprooms, and wine bars. Lower value than all-beverages but still a significant asset.

Resort License

Resort & Recreational

Available to qualified resort facilities in Montana. Different transfer rules and valuation methodology. Requires specific operational criteria to maintain eligibility.

Catering Endorsement

Catering & Special Events

Endorsements attached to existing licenses that add value and operational flexibility. Important to buyers evaluating the full scope of what they're acquiring.

What Determines a Montana Liquor License's Value?

Several factors drive liquor license valuation in Montana:

License type — All-beverages licenses command the highest prices due to quota restrictions. County quota status — In counties near or at their population-based quota, license values are highest because new licenses cannot be issued. Missoula County's growth has maintained strong license values. Current business operations — A license attached to an operating, profitable business is worth more than a dormant or recently-transferred license. Physical location — Licenses tied to established, high-traffic locations carry location premium. Market timing — Buyer demand from out-of-state operators entering the Montana market has supported license values in recent years.

Important:

Montana liquor license transfers must be approved by the Montana Department of Revenue Liquor Control Division. The process involves application review, background checks, and a mandatory waiting period. An experienced broker manages this process to avoid delays and ensure compliance — mistakes in the transfer process can void a transaction or result in license suspension.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does a Montana liquor license transfer take?
The Montana Department of Revenue Liquor Control Division typically takes 60–120 days to process a license transfer application, depending on application completeness, background check timelines, and current workload. Planning for 90 days from executed purchase agreement to license transfer completion is prudent. An experienced broker ensures the application is complete and correctly filed to avoid unnecessary delays.
Can a Montana liquor license be sold separately from the business?
Yes — in most cases a Montana liquor license can be transferred independently of the underlying business or real property. However, the license must be transferred to a qualified buyer who meets DOR requirements, and the license cannot simply be held dormant indefinitely. The specifics depend on license type and circumstances. This is one of the reasons working with an experienced broker matters.
What is a Montana all-beverages license worth?
Values vary significantly based on county, location, and market conditions. In Missoula and Western Montana, all-beverages licenses have historically traded in a wide range depending on quota status and operational factors.
