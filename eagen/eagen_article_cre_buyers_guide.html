What Types of Commercial Real Estate Are Available in Missoula?

The Missoula commercial market encompasses several distinct asset classes, each with its own buyer pool, valuation methodology, and market dynamics:

Multifamily / Apartments

The most active segment of the Missoula investment market. Strong rental demand driven by the University of Montana, population growth, and limited new supply has kept vacancy historically low and cap rates compressed.

Retail

Ranges from national-credit NNN properties to local strip centers and single-tenant buildings. The Reserve Street corridor and downtown Missoula are the primary retail submarkets.

Office

Medical office has remained resilient. General office has faced headwinds from remote work trends, though Missoula's market has been less affected than larger metros.

Industrial / Flex

Consistently tight inventory with very low vacancy. Missoula's industrial base serves regional distribution, trades, and light manufacturing. New product is limited by available land.

Mixed-Use

Downtown Missoula has a concentration of mixed-use properties with street-level retail and upper-floor residential or office. These are among the most sought-after assets in the market.

Licensed Establishments

Properties with Montana liquor licenses or gaming licenses carry significant additional value from the license itself. These transactions require specialized knowledge of Montana's license transfer process.

How Is Commercial Property Valued in Missoula?

Commercial real estate is valued differently than residential property. Rather than comparing bedroom counts and square footage, commercial valuation is driven by income — specifically, the Net Operating Income (NOI) the property produces, divided by the capitalization rate (cap rate) appropriate for that property type and location.

The formula: Value = NOI ÷ Cap Rate

A property producing $100,000 in annual NOI in a market where comparable properties are trading at a 6% cap rate is worth approximately $1.67 million. The same property in a market where cap rates have compressed to 5% is worth $2 million. Understanding cap rate trends in Missoula — which vary by asset class and have shifted materially over the past several years — is essential for any serious buyer.

Owner-occupied commercial properties — where the buyer intends to operate their own business in the building — are valued differently, often as a blend of income value and comparable sales, weighted toward what the market will bear for similar properties regardless of what the current owner is generating.

Key point for buyers: Cap rates are not static. They move with interest rates, local market conditions, and investor demand. A property that was fairly priced at a 5.5% cap rate two years ago may be overpriced at the same cap rate today if the market has shifted. Your broker should provide current comparable transaction data — not just current listings — to support any valuation analysis.

How Do You Finance Commercial Real Estate in Missoula?

Commercial real estate financing in Missoula is available through several channels:

  • Conventional commercial loans — offered by local and regional banks. Typically require 25 to 35 percent down, with terms of 5 to 10 years amortized over 20 to 25 years. Rates are tied to index rates (Prime, SOFR, or Treasury benchmarks) plus a spread.
  • SBA 504 loans — for owner-occupied commercial real estate. Allow buyers to acquire with as little as 10 percent down in some cases, with below-market fixed rates on the SBA portion. Excellent for business owners buying their own building.
  • SBA 7(a) loans — more commonly used for business acquisitions that include real property. More flexible than 504 but with floating rates and shorter amortization.
  • Seller financing — more common in Missoula than in larger markets, particularly for smaller transactions where conventional lenders are less interested. Sellers carry back a portion of the purchase price, often at terms that make the deal financeable when conventional lending alone would not.
  • 1031 exchange proceeds — a significant portion of commercial buyers in Missoula are 1031 exchange buyers placing proceeds from sales elsewhere. These buyers often move quickly and can close without financing contingencies, making them highly attractive to sellers.

What Does the Missoula Commercial Market Look Like for Buyers Right Now?

Missoula's commercial market has experienced meaningful cap rate compression over the past decade as out-of-state investor interest has increased, driven by population growth, quality of life, and the relative affordability of Missoula compared to larger Mountain West markets like Bozeman, Denver, and Salt Lake City. The result is a market where well-located, well-leased assets are difficult to acquire at historically attractive cap rates — but where opportunities still exist for buyers who are well-positioned and working with a broker who has access to off-market inventory.

The most active buyer segments in the current market are 1031 exchange buyers placing proceeds from West Coast sales, local owner-operators buying their own buildings, and out-of-state investors — particularly from California and the Pacific Northwest — attracted to Missoula's fundamentals and relative value. Competition for quality assets can be significant, particularly in the multifamily segment.

For buyers focused on Missoula commercial real estate, the path to finding the right property usually runs through a broker relationship rather than a public listing search. The best properties in Missoula are frequently sold before they ever appear on LoopNet or CoStar — either through direct broker-to-broker relationships or through a seller's broker reaching out to their established buyer network.
