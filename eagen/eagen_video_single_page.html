After 34 years in the Missoula commercial real estate market, Jessie Eagen has seen every cycle — the boom years, the corrections, and the long flat stretches in between. In this video, he shares why he remains bullish on Missoula as an investment market and what the fundamentals say about where values are heading.

Missoula has emerged as one of the most sought-after small-city investment markets in the Mountain West, attracting capital from California, the Pacific Northwest, and beyond. The reasons are structural — population growth, a strong university presence, limited developable land, and an economy that has diversified well beyond a single employer or industry. Jessie breaks down each of these factors and explains what they mean in practical terms for investors looking at the market today.

Questions From This Video

Why is Missoula attractive to out-of-state commercial real estate investors?

Missoula offers population growth, a diversified local economy, constrained land supply, and cap rates that — while compressed from historical levels — still compare favorably to larger Mountain West markets. Out-of-state investors, particularly from California and the Pacific Northwest, have been active buyers in Missoula for the past decade.

What types of commercial property are investors most interested in in Missoula?

Multifamily has been the most competitive segment, driven by strong rental demand and low vacancy. Industrial has limited supply and consistent demand. Net lease retail with credit tenants attracts passive investors. 1031 exchange buyers often look across all categories depending on their exchange amount and timeline.

Is now a good time to invest in Missoula commercial real estate?

The window for deep value relative to larger markets has narrowed — but Missoula's fundamentals remain strong. Investors who work with a broker who has access to off-market inventory can still find opportunities that don't appear in public searches. Timing matters less than positioning.
